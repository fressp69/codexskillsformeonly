# Worked plan — ridge watchtower

**Brief.** A hilltop landmark should guide a traveler to a lookout without flight or block placement. Its silhouette is a broad plinth, a narrower tower and an overhanging signal roof. Use real masonry throughout. The entrance faces the trail; the open observation bay faces the valley.

**Set out the plan.** Center a 17 × 17 stepped plinth on (0,0). Use a square tower with outer walls at x,z = ±5 and floor levels at relative Y = 0, 4 and 8 (standing levels). Put a three-cell opening in the south wall, cut windows above sill height, and carry four heavier corner piers up through the observation deck. Use tuff for the wall field, stone brick for piers, and dark oak under the roof eaves. Repeat the corner profile at each landing; reserve copper for the signal brazier.

**Resolve the section before detailing.** Run a four-riser stair west-to-east along the inner north wall, with a two-cell landing, then return on an adjacent flight to the lookout. Reserve both flights and their headroom before filling upper floors. A stair beginning at relative level 0 must arrive on level 4. Set riser block states from the local direction and rotate them with the entrance. Build a pyramidal roof above standing level 11 using successively inset square rings. Keep the centerline above the stair exit clear.

**Fit the ridge.** Survey the plinth and approach stairs. Use the highest safe feet level for the terrace if each exposed edge can reach solid bearing within three fill cells. Check the downhill gate separately and extend its entry stair until it meets the trail. Reject the site if the stairs run beyond the permitted volume. This is an extension of the Nexus deck rule that explicitly solves the gate-to-landscape transition.

**Acceptance.** Every orientation has a connected route from trail to lookout, headroom over each tread and a valley sightline above the parapet. Compare the downhill elevation before adding vegetation. Simulate partial placement between stair and roof phases and verify that reconstruction preserves stair facing.

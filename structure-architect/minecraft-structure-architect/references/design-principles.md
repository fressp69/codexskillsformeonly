# Proportion, palette and refinement

| Decision | Nexus evidence | Transfer and limit | Practical application |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size a bay from its use | Eight equal bays surround an open ritual center. Cross-shaped pillar feet use neighboring cells, and four alternating edges have openings. | Subtract the projected width of both adjoining supports from the bay span, then measure free cells in the snapped plan. A radius alone cannot guarantee a gateway. | Lay out a fortress gate with two players passing side by side before widening its towers. |
| Make the load path visible | Solid deepslate foot, narrower shaft, inward grates, copper cap, then a visible air gap to the crystal. | Change section at the footing, joint and crown. Keep the walking lane outside protruding brackets. | A factory column can use a concrete plinth, iron shaft and copper bearing under a real roof beam. |
| Control the focal hierarchy | Dark floor field, polished rim, radial copper inlays, central lodestone and a suspended multicomponent heart. | Spend contrast where players orient or act. Repeated bright features need a quieter field. | A dungeon junction uses a pale lintel above the exit and muted stone along service passages. |
| Give material changes a rule | Tiles alternate with bricks by coordinate pattern. Copper marks joints and energy paths; waxed variants preserve the intended finish. | Choose field, edge, joint and accent materials before adding variation. A repeating pattern can read as stripes at distance. | Examine a single full-size bay in daylight and darkness before copying it twelve times. |
| Make symmetry legible and useful | All vertices repeat, while alternate edges change function. Inlays draw the eye inward. | Repeat a structural motif and vary it where access, drainage or machinery requires it. | One broken aqueduct bay can tell a history if its abutments and diverted channel explain the gap. |

**Recommended refinement pass.** Read the silhouette first at discovery distance. At the threshold, check that elevation changes reveal the destination. At player eye height, look for cap blocks obscuring the objective and decoration narrowing escape routes. On a hillside inspect the downhill underside. Remove detail that conceals a joint or route. For asymmetrical builds, repeat bay dimensions, roof pitch or sill heights to establish continuity without imposing radial symmetry.
